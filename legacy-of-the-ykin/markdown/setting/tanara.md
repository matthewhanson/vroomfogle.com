# Tanara

*Note: Canon sources for Tanara are included in the Master Atlas, Jaiman: Land of Twilight, and The Cloudlords of Tanara. This gives a summary of pertinent details and expands upon areas not covered in canon.*

## Geography of Tanara

Tanara lies on the southeastern coast of Jaiman, bordered by Rhakhaan to the west, Urulan to the east (across the Bay of Urulan), and the Straits of Meluria to the south. Its shore is indented by the Bay of Tanara, while the Grey Mountains form a weather‑beaten wall along much of its western edge. Strong Essænce flows and stormy seas shape travel, trade, and culture here.

### Key Regions & Landmarks

* **Bay of Tanara** – The great inlet on Tanara’s east. Most sea traffic that reaches Tanara enters here.  
* **Amrillar Islands** – Low, reef‑strewn isles scattered within the Bay of Tanara; home to many Sulini fishers and pilots. Local waters are hazardous to outsiders.  
* **Sulini Coast** – The southern littoral along the Straits of Meluria and the lower shores of the Bay of Tanara. Warm, windy, and salt‑sprayed; dotted with stilt‑villages, temples to Numa, and vineyards on nearby low hills.  
* **Kaid** – Major port on Tanara’s eastern shore of the Bay of Tanara (not on the Sulini Coast). It is the primary foreign gateway for overland caravans and limited sea trade.  
* **Durakaan (Dúranaki Forests & Caverns)** – Heavily wooded uplands and vast cave‑complexes north and northwest of the Myri farmlands. Entrances are hidden beneath the forest canopy; the Dúranaki seldom show themselves by day.  
* **Myri Uplands** – Cool, rolling interior hills with scattered stone farmsteads and towns; most Myri clans dwell here away from the coasts and high mountains.  
* **Y’kin (Yinka) Foothills** – Cliff‑side settlements and terrace‑fields in the southern foothills of the Grey Mountains; the theocracy presses north and east for land and captives.

### Lands & Waters

* **Mountain Walls:** Western crest >12,000’ tapering to ~5,000’ along eastward spurs; sea‑facing cliffs north and south.  
* **Highlands:** Rocky foothills fall to fertile, stream‑laced farms; ideal for grains and grazing.  
* **Forests:** The great northern forest hosts outsized growth, warmer understory, and high humidity.  
* **Coast:** Low ridge to broad beaches broken by black volcanic outcrops; offshore coral ribbons and knife‑rock shoals.  
* **Interior Waters:** Glacial lakes and spring‑fed rivers interconnect; spring floods are common.  
* **Sea Conditions:** Bay shelter damps wave energy, but straits toward Urulan are treacherous with relentless winds and current rips.

## Climate & Weather

* **Climate Bands:**  
  * **Coast:** Mild temps, small annual swing; frequent mists.  
  * **Highlands:** Warm summers; hard freezes and snow in winter.  
  * **Southern Littoral:** Windy and comparatively dry in late summer; storm‑prone springs and autumns.  
* **Seasonal Tendencies:**  
  * **Ilarin (winter):** Snow common inland/highlands; coastal squalls.
  * **Tiralin (spring):** Heavy rains; floods; morning fog inland.  
  * **Arin (summer):** Warm coast; thunderstorms highland; south trends dry.  
  * **Norin (autumn):** Storm tracks shift; strong coastal gales.  
  * **Akirin (fall):** First hard frosts; clearer air windows.  
 

## Peoples of Tanara

Four primary cultures dominate Tanara, each shaped by geography, trade, and belief. Though descended from ancient Jaimani stock, their isolation and philosophies have driven them along distinct paths. Together these peoples define Tanara’s fragile balance—commerce and faith bound by geography and the unpredictable breath of the Essænce.

* **Languages:** A common trade dialect bridges distinct native tongues; Dúranaki script is complex and symbolic, while Myri language is lyrical and rhythmic.  
* **Art & Music:** Sulini sea chants, Dúranaki harmonic chimes, Myri drums and songs of lineage, Y’kin hymns echoing from basalt temples.  
* **Relations:** Sulini act as intermediaries; Dúranaki trade artifacts for food; Myri hold moral authority; Y’kin embody the ever-present threat of zeal and conquest.

### Sulini

Seafarers and merchants of the southern coast and Amrillar Islands. Their stilted villages and shallow-draft ships thrive amid reefs and shifting currents. Sulini culture prizes adaptability and negotiation over warfare; family and guild networks define authority more than lineage. The **Princedom of Sulinor** governs by trade oath and temple charter. Their devotion to **Numa**, Avatar of **Shaal**, infuses daily life with maritime ritual—tide-readings, shell amulets, and headland shrines. Sulini sailors are Tanara’s link to Urulan and Emer, serving as pilots, smugglers, and sometimes spies.

### Dúranaki

Dwelling in the caverns and forests of **Durakaan**, the Dúranaki are master artisans and keepers of hidden lore. Their **Jarhaad Council of Seven Families** rules through knowledge and craft monopoly. They excel in alchemy, metallurgy, and mirrorwork—producing the famed **Keron-alloy** and glass infused with Essænce. Their society values intellect and restraint, but beneath this civility lies the moral stain of **conditioned Myri thralls**, psychic bonds used for labor and protection. Dúranaki faith is philosophical: gods are archetypes of perfection, not beings to be worshipped. Though reclusive, they trade discreetly with the Sulini and sometimes aid reformist causes.

### Myri

Agrarian clans of the uplands and valleys, the Myri are hardy, pious, and communal. Their villages cluster around stone halls and terraced fields, each led by a **Keviik** (chieftain) chosen by consensus. Oral law and song preserve ancestry and justice. Their pantheon—**Phaon**, **Illila**, **Allanda**, **Keo**, and **Davix**—governs sun, moon, harvest, wind, and guardianship. Seasonal festivals such as **Rethlorin** blend worship, trade, and matchmaking. Myri craftsmanship favors stone, pottery, and woven art; their sense of honor and hospitality runs deep. 

### Y’kin (Yinka)

The Y’kin—called “Yinka” in older sources—are a theocratic cliff‑people whose terrace cities cling to the southern foothills of the Grey Mountains, looking out over the Straits of Meluria and the lower Bay of Tanara. By the late Third Era they are reduced and embattled, driven into Tanara by larger powers, yet still dangerous along the marches. Y’kin precincts control perilous routes through the foothills and levy tolls, escorts, and temple “licenses” where they can. They are rigorously devout and expansion‑minded , their fortress‑temples crowning black basalt escarpments and their law carried by spear cohorts and priest‑judges.

T Y’kin remember themselves as children of the **Ochu**, migrants who fled oppression in northern Emer and crossed the Melurian Straits to the southern shores of Tanara. They made first harbor along the Melurian Straits, then pressed north and west into southern Tanara, raising stair‑roads and irrigated ledges and, for a season, projecting influence as far as the marches of Rhakhaan. Early encounters were with the **Sulini** along the coasts and the **Myri** of the interior uplands; the **Dúranaki** (originally the **Zori**) arrived centuries later, bringing new trade and tensions to the northern woods. By **5904–5950 T.E.**, pressure from Rhakhaan under **Empress Italana** pushed Y’kin communities **east and north** into Tanara, accelerating their consolidation along the southern Grey foothills.

Their society is a lattice of precincts ruled by Priest‑Lords, but its customs are shaped as much by the household and the school as by the altar. The Y’kin confess a cosmology of return—souls reborn upward through merit—and bind that doctrine to the cult of **Yugal**, the radiant judge whose carved face is deliberately Y’kin. In Tanara the priesthood teaches that **Yugal is Yugal**: he may be called by other names elsewhere, yet such aliases are the concern of outsiders and of little import to the faithful. His law is light, and impurity is purged by blood and oath. Sacrifice—animals as a matter of course, humans on the highest eves—seals covenants and consecrates judgments.

Population is a civic duty. There is no formal marriage; sexuality is framed first as a communal obligation to people the terraces; dormitory courts. Matron‑led registries coordinate matches and infant care. The result is dense, multi‑generational compounds built into the cliff face, bound by shared granaries and cisterns.

Women hold conspicuous authority in education, logistics, and administration; **rarely, they are admitted to holy orders**. Exceptional priestesses serve by dispensation and are more often jurists, theologians, or keepers of law than battlefield celebrants. **Priestess Shemere** is the most prominent example: a senior jurist seated on the **Inner Conclave**, a council of **twelve** who stand immediately below the Arch Priest‑Lord in ritual precedence and policy. Funerary practice reflects these roles: men of lawful service receive inscribed stelae at the terrace edge; women who have borne many children or endowed works of peace—or who have taken vows—are honored in shrines and ancestor niches within the precinct walls. **Bodies found outside the precincts are left to the elements, while deaths within a city or in battle are incinerated without extended rite—the spirit’s rebirth is the rite.**

Law is oath and spectacle. Courts convene in sun‑paved forums; punishments range from branded penances and indenture to ritual execution when Orhan stands full and Charôn is in the priest’s ascendant hour. Cohorts led by secular **marsars** fight for the temples: shielded spears for the field, light troops for cliff war, and Essænce‑trained deacons who wield blinding lances on the line. **Historically** the Y’kin have taken captives in war—binding Myri to terrace labor or ransoming Sulini—but **a millennium of uneasy peace** left **few captives** within Tanara by the year 6000. With tensions rising yet the truce intact in late Winter 6049, some precincts whisper of reviving those practices, though policy is not yet uniform. Free households continue to tithe labor to precinct works. **The Ixtal program’s long‑term ‘Plan’ is the cultivation of an elite, doctrinally pure army by shaping recruits from childhood.**

**Physique & Dress.** Y’kin men average **5ʹ4ʺ–5ʹ6ʺ**, stocky; women near **5ʹ0ʺ**; **mahogany skin** and **brown eyes**. Cold seasons favor **heavy wool and furs**; summers bring **belted cotton robes** and **calf-length tunics**. **Red, yellow, and rust** dominate their palette. Both sexes favor **gold jewelry** with **tinted glass/crystal inlays** and striking **cosmetics** (bold eye shadow; face‑paint stripes). Priests wear **full-length red robes with grey trim** and a **heavy gold amulet** of office.

For generations a precarious peace obtained along the southern marches, a chain of market‑host rights kept by custom more than treaty. In **late Winter 6049 T.E.** the compact still holds: Y’kin cohorts **muster** on terrace grounds and **stage supplies** near the lower fords but have **not** crossed. Border markets thin, Myri clans fortify watch‑steads, Sulini pilots harden the reef‑routes, and rumors of Cloudlords in the Vale of Merisia serve as rallying words on both sides.

## Creatures & Domesticated Species

* **Tameki (pet):** Blue‑black, long‑fingered arboreal companions of Dúranaki elites; agile and inquisitive.  
* **Ozora (semi‑domesticate):** Seal‑like Sulini helpers; net‑untanglers, fish‑finders, loud sentinels.
* **Cliff Cats:** Grey cougars; livestock raiders; rarely attack men unless desperate.  
* **Shaskan:** Small winged drakes; venomous bite; sun‑baskers of rocky lowlands.  
* **Bears:** Black and brown; the latter dangerously aggressive at range.  
* **Grey Bats:** Cave swarms with anesthetizing venom; lethal in numbers.  
* **Garks:** Arboreal, tool‑using primatoids; virulent hatred of mannish folk, especially Dúranaki.  

## Trade, Currency & Economy

Tanara’s economy is defined by isolation and interdependence. Each culture produces goods shaped by its environment and temperament, creating rich opportunities for barter, smuggling, and adventure hooks.

**Sulini Trade Network:** The Sulini dominate maritime commerce. Their ports on the Sulini Coast and Kaid handle nearly all foreign goods that enter Tanara—fine cloth, spices, and tools from Urulan, exchanged for Tanaran wine, fish oil, and crafted glass. Their sailors double as merchants and spies, and Kaid’s markets host exotic trade fairs twice yearly.

**Dúranaki Craft and Coin:** The Dúranaki mint fine silver and copper coinage, widely accepted for its purity. Their artisans are renowned for precision metalwork, alchemical tools, and the rare Keron-alloy blades. Trade caravans from Durakaan are few but laden with high-value items—mirrors, instruments, jewelry—sold through Sulini intermediaries. Paper ‘debt notes’ circulate among merchants of the Seven Families, serving as promissory currency.

**Myri Barter Economy:** The Myri rely primarily on barter and clan credit, exchanging grain, livestock, pottery, and herbs. Traveling peddlers act as middlemen, carrying Myri produce to Sulini ports or Dúranaki buyers. During festivals like Rethlorin, temporary trade fairs double as religious celebrations and political meetings. Silver and gold hold symbolic rather than monetary value here.

**Y’kin Theocracy and Tribute:** The Y’kin economy is militarized and centrally controlled by the Temple of Yugal. Agricultural output is tithed to the priesthood, and captured goods—especially Myri slaves and Sulini craft—are redistributed as temple property. The gold coinage of the Holy Court is rare but ornate, embossed with Yugal’s sun-mark sigil.

**Foreign Exchange:** True foreign trade is limited. Only the most daring captains from Urulan and beyond risk the storms of the Bay of Tanara. Smugglers thrive, and strange artifacts surface occasionally in Kaid’s black markets. Sel-kai coinage (from the great trading city in northern Emer) is accepted throughout Kaid and by Sulini merchants, prized for its uniform weight and minting purity; it serves as the de facto standard for large transactions and foreign exchange.
