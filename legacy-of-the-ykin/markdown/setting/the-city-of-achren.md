# Achren

Nestled along the coursing Cohoa river, Achren stands tall as a juxtaposition of its ancient roots and its tumultuous present. While the river splits it into eastern and western domains, it is the Bridge of Vows that truly ties the city together. In spring, the waters below roar with fury, transforming the gorge into a raging river, symbolizing the city's own tumultuous history. But as summer unfolds, the waters retreat, leaving in its wake an expanse of lush vegetation, verdant and teeming with life, representing hope and rebirth.

An architectural marvel imbued with powerful Essænce, the bridge does more than connect two city halves—it plays an instrumental role in shaping the city’s sociopolitical landscape in the absence of the Priest-Lords. The bridge serves as a silent witness to the promises made and the oaths sealed.

## In the time of the Priest-Lords

Achren, before the priesthood’s attention drifted elsewhere (Winter, 6049, at the opening of the war), was a city of stark contrasts, veiled in an omnipresent shroud of tension. Crime was rampant, and the streets echoed with the desperate cries of beggars and the whispered plots of criminals. The underworld held an iron grip on the city's pulse, yet it was a chaotic one. Stabbings in shadowed alleys, underhanded deals in dimly lit taverns, and swift justice dealt out by the gangs were all too common.

But above this maelstrom of illicit activities, the Priest-Lords watched with cold, disinterested eyes. On most days, they allowed the crime to flourish unchecked, as if the degradation and despair below were but an inconsequential backdrop to their grand designs. The underbelly of Achren, after all, served a purpose: it kept the masses distracted, divided, and weak.

Yet, when the Priest-Lords decided to act, their power was swift and brutal. Crackdowns were merciless. Streets would be awash with the blood of those who dared defy them, and entire criminal syndicates could vanish overnight. This heavy hand ensured that, while the underworld might fester and grow, it would never truly threaten the Priest-Lords' supremacy.

In the time of the Priest-Lords, Achren was a place of paradoxes: a city of immense power and profound weakness, of opulence and abject poverty, of order and chaos. It was a city that bore the scars of its rulers and the resilience of its inhabitants.

## The Bridge of Vows

Majestically spanning the Cohoa river, the Bridge of Vows stands as in distinct contrast to the city, pre-dating the Y'kin by many millenia. Crafted from an exquisite green-gray stone and assembled without the use of mortar, its very existence defies architectural convention. At each end, sculptures of cliff cats stand guard, their stony gazes seemingly evaluating every passerby, as if to remind them of the gravity of oaths taken upon the bridge's hallowed ground. But the bridge does more than just bind words; it evokes a deep sense of magnanimity in those who traverse it. Each step on its storied expanse not only connects two shores but also binds the hopes, dreams, and fates of all who cross, making it much more than a mere passage—it's a testament to Achren's past, present, and hopeful future.

The Bridge of Vows used to be the beating heart of Achren, a meeting point where trade negotiations and diplomatic talks unfurled. Its unique spirit not only ensured truthful dealings but also promotes a sense of fairness, leading to just and equitable agreements. For centuries, even before the rise and fall of the Priest-Lords, the bridge was the nexus of city life.

In truth, the bridge’s reputation has always been *larger* than what most citizens can prove. Long ago—far back into the city’s oldest stories—the bridge was said to have enforced vows made upon its stones. But that age passed. The key to the bridge’s full power (a mysterious gem) was lost, and for as long as anyone in living memory can confirm, the bridge carried only **minor latent effects**: a subtle confidence in those who crossed, a momentary sense of magnanimity of purpose, and a social pressure to deal fairly.

During their tenure, the Priest-Lords controlled access to the bridge and shaped the city around it. They effectively cultivated (and policed) **two Achrens**, using monitored crossings and controlled points of passage to keep commerce and influence flowing only as they wished. In doing so, they also kept the deepest truths about the bridge close to their chests—letting the public believe in a romantic “Bridge of Vows,” while ensuring no one could easily test its legends.

## The Priest-Lords Look Away

Achren wasn’t “freed” because every Priest-Lord marched out in a single dramatic exodus. It was left alone because the war demanded attention, resources, and ritual focus elsewhere. The upper priesthood narrowed its gaze to larger fronts and grander concerns—leaving only a smaller contingent of priests in Achren, a bureaucracy that could be pressured, bribed, and intimidated.

After years of research the Locusts found their plan, and masterminded a scheme that used the Bridge of Vows to force a small circle of high-ranking priests (and their local agents) into non-interference—binding them to step back from Achren’s day-to-day rule and to remain silent about *why*.

To do it, they stole the missing keystone: a mysterious gem held by the **Afflicted** (the undercity beggar-network; see below). Known (or at least suspected) by the Locusts, but **unbeknownst to most Afflicted**, the gem was the bridge’s lost catalyst. The Locusts placed it into the Bridge of Vows—*restoring what had been dormant for ages*—and the bridge’s power to **enforce vows and deals made upon it** awakened in full.

In the aftermath, rumors (and then certainty) about the bridge’s awakened powers spread quickly. Almost overnight, Achren transformed from a place of distrust and subterfuge to a city founded upon binding agreements. This evolution had several far-reaching implications:

* **Shift in Power Dynamics**: No longer were the mighty and influential the sole arbitrators of the city's fate. Any group, regardless of size or influence, could use the bridge to make binding agreements, challenging traditional hierarchies and making room for marginalized groups like the Y'sari to assert their stance.  
* **Exploitation**: With great power comes great responsibility—and the potential for misuse. Crafty individuals could manipulate unsuspecting victims into undesirable vows, seek loopholes in phrasing, or use coercion as a means to an end.  
* **Legitimization of Faction Deals**: The bridge's inherent promise of honesty meant factions could form unexpected alliances, secure in their newfound trust. Smaller groups could now join forces, challenging the status quo and creating a more diversified power landscape.  
* **Trustworthiness as Currency**: In the treacherous world of Achren, trust became the new gold standard. Factions that consistently honored their bridge vows elevated their reputation, opening doors to lucrative deals and alliances.  
* **Rise of Mediators and Brokers**: With the bridge's abilities known, a new profession emerged: individuals specializing in crafting foolproof agreements. These brokers of trust became indispensable, wielding their expertise to ensure every pact was unambiguous and just.  
* **Elimination of Double-Dealing**: The duplicitous games that once marred faction relationships were rendered obsolete. The bridge's uncompromising enforcement of promises meant a more transparent, straightforward way of doing business.

The larger Y'kin state—the distant bureaucrats, generals, and upper priesthood focused on the war—remains largely unaware (or willfully incurious) about the true events transpiring within Achren’s walls. Meanwhile, the smaller priestly presence that still exists in the city is fragmented and compromised: influenced, bribed, and intimidated into letting Achren govern itself so long as quotas are met and the war effort gets its men and resources.

Amid these transformations, the bitterness of some Afflicted was palpable. The theft of the gem—an artifact many of them did not fully understand—became a wound that never quite closed. It also created a fault line between **Afflicted** and **Locusts**: allied on some nights, adversaries on others.

As Achren navigates this uncharted territory, only time will reveal if the city's commitment to trustworthiness and transparency will stand firm or if the shadows of the past will once again darken its streets.

### Vow Crafters: The New Power Brokers

Amidst this landscape, the Vow Crafters—once a niche profession—have risen to prominence. These specialists, adept at molding words and ensuring that every vow taken on the bridge is unambiguous and airtight, have become indispensable. As the bridge dictates the rhythm of commerce and agreements in Achren, the Vow Crafters play the maestros, orchestrating each transaction with precision.

In practice, some Vow Crafters are also **fixers**—people who can quietly arrange meetings, verify identities, and enforce “clean phrasing” when factions are one bad sentence away from bloodshed.

## The Afflicted

Beneath the life of Achren lies a hidden world, a sprawling network of catacombs, tunnels, and abandoned structures. This realm is home to the beggars and thieves for those unwanted from society, the diseased, the maimed. These are the Afflicted, which provides a place of refuge for some, but is also a hub of activity, faith, and covert operations. It also contains treasures from lost times.

The Afflicted are not just passive recipients of charity; they are active agents with their own secret dealings and traditions.

* **Changeling Rumor**: A legend exists that the Afflicted switch their children with normal babies from the City.  
* **Secret Helpers**: While they sometimes burglarize homes and shops, they steal not valuables but information. They occasionally return to help those who have shown them kindness.  
* **Mastery Over Pain**: Having many among them who constantly deal with pain, the beggars have learned the arts of nerve manipulation, which can be used both for torture and self-defense.  
* **Information Network**: The beggars have a sophisticated system of collecting and analyzing every piece of news or rumor that flows through the City. This intel not only keeps them safe but also gives them leverage in various dealings.  
* **A Dark Leader**: Their leader, whom they call Y’sari, is an avatar of Bugaluth, Lord of Pestilence who appears as a half-blind cripple.  He is corrupting Achren’s unwanted and building an armor of undead servants.

### Key Figures

* **Kuknal**: Known as the beggar King, Kuknal is a physically strong individual born of Ysraiget lineage. Despite his physical challenges, he is a wise and effective leader. He values information greatly and encourages integration with the Upworld for intelligence gathering. Myre often makes calculated decisions based on the welfare of the beggars and future scenarios.  
* **Nalini**: The beggar Queen and Myre's consort, Talissa was once a courtesan who was horrifically maimed. She wears a black lace half-mask to hide her scars but remains a stunning beauty. Talissa is the mysterious Lace Lady rumored to switch babies with regular ones. She has a burning desire to discover the identity of the person responsible for her disfigurement and has employed the Afflicted’s espionage network to this end.  
* **The Captain**: Formerly a Siege-master who suffered a disabling injury, The Captain has been instrumental in fortifying the Undercity warrens. He is highly protective of the Afflicted, especially those more aligned with Y’sari and views his work as a divine mission.  
* **Taloc**: A tall youth with a unique take on religious matters, Taloc is an important advisor to Kuknal. Born with a club foot, Taloc is physically handicapped, but his intellect and knowledge about the City's criminal structure make him indispensable.  
* **Xochipilli**: A wiry, stooped beggar, Xochipilli’s sun-baked clay-colored skin stretched taut over pronounced bones. His hollow cheeks and stark cheekbones frame murky brown eyes clouded by cataracts, yet still burning with resilience. Hardship has etched lines deep into his face, further accentuated by the blemishes of the Affliction. His faded gray hair, once black, hangs in unkempt tresses, while his gnarled hands, scarred and calloused, speak of enduring struggles. A patchwork cloak drapes his frame, and a worn-out leather band, inscribed with glyphs, encircles his wrist — a silent testament to a bygone era or perhaps a beacon of hope.  
* **Tlalocan**: An older Y'kin soldier, bears the weary marks of time and a long-ago clash with the Garks — notably his missing left arm. His graying hair is pulled back tightly, with a few bone trinkets hinting at past battles. While his exterior is gruff and his voice has grown raspy with age, the intensity in his eyes hasn't dimmed. Those in the ranks might see him as just another war-hardened trainer, but unbeknownst to many, Tlalocan secretly aids the Afflicted, funneling them information from within the military's walls.

## Places of Note

### Kolat’s Forbidden Emporium

Kolat’s Forbidden Emporium is a consequence of the Bridge’s awakening: a pop-up market made permanent. When the Bridge of Vows began enforcing deals and the city’s commerce shifted toward public, binding agreements, a window opened for goods that *used* to move only through whispered hands.

In the bustling streets of Y'kin, amidst buildings boasting the region's distinctive architectural style with those iconic red roofs, stands Kolat's Forbidden Emporium. The edifice—once a bustling wainwright shop—now houses a curated sprawl of curios, art, and books.

The truth (known to anyone with underworld contacts) is that very little of this inventory is "new." The same magical trinkets, outlawed texts, and scandalous art could be found on the black market for years. Kolat simply recognized the moment: with trust becoming currency and deals increasingly struck in the open, he began selling black-market wares openly—pricing them higher, presenting them as “legitimate,” and relying on the new order to keep rivals and thieves cautious.

The main structure opens into a vast courtyard, dotted with several outbuildings that once served as storage for wagon parts, tools, and timber. Now, each of these structures houses a chaotic assortment of items. Some are categorized by origin, some by age, and some seem to be grouped by Kolat's own inscrutable logic.

The courtyard itself is a mosaic of items laid out without any discernible order. There's a large intricately carved statue sitting next to a pile of hand-woven textiles, while just a few steps away, an antique mirror reflects the image of an ornate, ancient-looking chandelier suspended from a makeshift stand. The ground is littered with rugs showcasing designs from various cultures, and in some spots, you can still see the imprints of wagon wheels, a nod to the building's previous life.

As you maneuver through the emporium, the scent of dried herbs mingles with the musty aroma of old books. Here, herbal concoctions are displayed in abundance, their vibrant colors and distinct aromas adding another layer to the sensory overload. These aren't your typical potions but rather a testament to Y'kin's rich tradition of herbal knowledge.

Walking through Kolat's emporium feels like a treasure hunt. Among the seemingly random assortment, the discerning eye might find a piece of art, an ancient manuscript, or a relic of a bygone era. And while many of the items might seem mundane at first glance, each holds a story, waiting for the right person to unearth it.

In Y'kin, where the past intertwines with the present, Kolat's Forbidden Emporium stands as a testament to the city's evolving identity, from a hub of wagon craftsmanship to a melting pot of art, history, and herbal wisdom. The chaotic charm of this place is undeniable, and those who venture in are bound to leave with a piece of history, a trinket, or at the very least, an unforgettable memory.

Visitors to The Forbidden Emporium are often warned to handle these curios with care, for their powers and histories are as enigmatic as they are entrancing.
