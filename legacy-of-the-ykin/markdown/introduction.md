# Introduction

There are few names in Tanara that stir more unease than the Y’kin. To most, they are little more than a warning from history — a people consumed by faith, whose cities turned to dust and whose memory was buried under fear. Everyone knows how their story ends. What no one remembers is how it truly began — or what was lost along the way.

*Legacy of the Y’kin* is a campaign about the forgotten people: — when devotion shaped daily life, ambition drove great works, and rebellion took root in small, dangerous ways. It is not the story of a single band of heroes, but a living chronicle told through many lives. Over the years of play, players will take on **multiple characters**, each drawn into the tide of events in their own way. Some will cross paths, others will fade into legend — their choices weaving together into the broader history of a kingdom fated to fall.

The Y’kin are at the heart of this tale, but not its only voice. Myri farmers, Sulini sailors, Dúranaki wanderers — all have roles to play. Your characters may come from any of these peoples, yet the Y’kin will always remain present: as allies, adversaries, ancestors, or the quiet echo that binds the tale together.

This campaign seeks to portray the Y’kin not just as historical casualties, but as key players. It embraces the known end — because within that inevitability lies its depth. Every act of courage, faith, or failure becomes part of understanding *why* the Y’kin fell, and what of them might still endure. This is the legacy of the Y’kin.

## The Setting

* **Begins:** Y’kin lands, southern Tanara (Jaiman), in **6049 T.E.**
* **Focus:** Y’kin war of aggression against the **Myri**
* **Locations:** Primarily Tanara (with potential forays into **Urulan**, **Rhakhaan**, or beyond)
* **Starting characters:** Elite Y’kin assassins hailing from the **Ixtal** region.
* **Shifting loyalties:** As the campaign progresses, players may grapple with the weight of their initial allegiances, reconsidering their stance toward the Y’kin Priest-Lords.
* **Stable of characters:** Players will switch between different characters as the group(s) shift over time, with characters going in and out.

## Why This Campaign Exists

I’ve always loved *Shadow World*. It’s this incredible setting — detailed, mysterious, and alive — where every line in the timeline feels like a story waiting to happen. There’s so much texture to it, from the ancient ruins and lost technologies to the way magic seeps into every part of the world. But what really makes *Shadow World* special is that, for all its richness, there are still blank spaces. Places, people, and moments that invite you to step in and fill them.

The Y’kin were one of those spaces. In canon, they’re mostly treated as a villain race — a fanatical theocracy standing in contrast to the “civilized” peoples of Tanara. But that’s just one snapshot at one moment in history. No culture starts that way. I wanted to dig deeper: to show how a people could evolve from scholars and artists to zealots, how good intentions can twist over generations, and how even within a corrupted system there are still those who resist or remember something better. The Y’kin deserved more than to just be the bad guys — they deserved a story.

This campaign is my way of telling that story. It spans about **five years** in-world starting in 6049 T.E., following *Shadow World’s* established timeline. But it’s not about one party of heroes moving in a straight line. It’s about a **tapestry of characters** — Y’kin and outsiders, soldiers and scholars, zealots and heretics — whose lives intersect as Tanara teeters on the edge of collapse. Players will come and go, new characters will rise while others fall, and sometimes entire arcs will pass before threads reconnect. The only rule is simple: there are **always Y’kin** somewhere in the story, even if they aren’t the same ones who began it.

The tone leans gritty and grounded — politics, faith, and survival all matter — but the extraordinary still cuts through. *Shadow World* has always balanced the mundane with the mythic, and this campaign does the same. There will be artifacts, ancient powers, and moments that feel larger than life. Characters won’t always be balanced in strength or influence; sometimes someone will take on a role more like Gandalf — stepping in when needed, then fading from view. That unevenness isn’t a flaw; it’s part of the world’s texture. It’s how stories feel when they’re told from many angles instead of one.

## Notes for Game Masters

The **rotating-character structure** is what gives this campaign its rhythm. The story moves through people rather than around them.

* **Character creation is ongoing.** Players are constantly exploring new concepts — people they’d like to inhabit next. I work with each player to find how that idea could naturally emerge within the story’s threads, adjusting where needed to keep continuity intact.
* **New characters begin as NPCs.** Almost every player character first appears as an NPC — someone the party meets, fights alongside, or hears rumors about. Once the time feels right, the player steps into that role, keeping the transition feel natural.
* **Departures aren’t always deaths.** Sometimes a character dies, but often they simply move on to their own quest - deep library research, vanishing on pilgrimage, or living out their own arc offscreen. They might return later, changed by time and consequence.
* **Uneven power is intentional.** Some sessions may feature a mystic or warrior of near-legend alongside new recruits. Others focus entirely on ground-level perspectives. That imbalance mirrors the world’s reality and makes the story feel more like a living chronicle than a staged adventure.
* **The Shadow World timeline is ground truth**. Time moves forward regardless of player involvement, and events unfold whether characters act or stand aside. The world responds to those events, carrying consequences that shape future history, not just the current moment.

This structure creates a campaign that feels alive — less like a quest log and more like history unfolding.
